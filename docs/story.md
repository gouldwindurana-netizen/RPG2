# The Road Beyond the Bells

Original story proposal for RPG2. Written around Mara, Tavin, Iona, and Soren from the [combat brief](combat-design.md), with a short opening expedition that can become the first playable chapter.

Expanded campaign draft. The [side-quest collection](side-quests.md) adds optional regional stories and two-part personal quests for each hero. The main story below remains complete without them.

## Premise

Every evening, the bells keep the monsters outside the walls. Nobody asks what keeps the bells ringing.

Across the realm of Avel, night brings a dark ground fog filled with creatures called the Hush. Great bronze bells surround the towns with protective light. Between their walls, abandoned roads disappear beneath weeds and travelers race the sunset.

When a frontier bell falls silent, four hired travelers enter its underground workings to find a missing maintenance crew. They expect broken machinery. Instead, they discover a living man strapped into the bell's mechanism, unable to remember his daughter's name.

The bells burn human memories for power. Their waste drifts beyond the walls, feeding the very creatures they hold back.

The party cannot simply destroy them. Thousands of people would be exposed to the fog. To end the sacrifice, they must first make another way to live.

## Tone

A hopeful fantasy adventure with an unsettling mystery underneath it. The danger is real, but the world is worth saving: noisy markets, bad traveling songs, arguments over camp cooking, people who lend strangers their last blanket.

Memory loss is treated as an injury, not as proof that someone's life has ended. People who have lost memories still make decisions, form relationships, and deserve futures. The ending does not magically undo their losses.

## The world and its rules

- The night fog predates the great bells. Its original cause is not a secret god or a final villain; it is a dangerous part of this world.
- Bell light immediately repels the Hush within a large protected area. Feeding a bell destroys portions of a captive's autobiographical memory. Ordinary names, objects, and written records remain; the damage is to the person.
- The spent memories leave a residue that thickens the surrounding fog over time. Stopping a bell prevents further waste, but does not instantly clear existing fog or kill the creatures inside it.
- Ordinary spellcasting does **not** burn memories. A caster's reserves recover with rest. This supports the game's limited spell charges without making every healing spell a moral sacrifice.
- Small wards can be maintained by rotating groups of trained casters. They protect a camp or village, not a crowded city or an entire road. Their costs are labor, fatigue, supplies, and the need for replacements when someone falls ill.
- A few outlying communities already use these wards. Their method is neither a lost miracle nor a complete solution on its own. The campaign must connect their isolated shelters and make evacuation practical.
- A person may recover from exhaustion or the binding procedure, but burned memories cannot be restored from a surviving machine. Diaries can tell someone what happened; they cannot return the experience of remembering it.
- Great bells operate locally. Destroying one does not switch off the others. Preparing replacement shelters and retiring the regional feeding chambers is required campaign work; Veyr becomes the last active captive-fed bell before the finale.

## People who travel through the story

**Bessa, the Reedbank keeper**, runs the camp with a ledger, a kettle, and a formidable ability to remember who has not eaten. She initially trusts the bell administration because its ration wagons kept the camp alive. After the rescue, she organizes Reedbank's relocation and becomes a coordinator of roadside shelters. She remains willing to argue with the heroes when their plans overlook ordinary needs.

**Leth, the Dunmere clerk**, first appears correcting a travel permit while the party waits in a queue. He has been copying discrepancies in the transport accounts for months, but needs a way to protect his coworkers before he exposes them. His recurring work is practical: duplicating evidence, locating prisoners, and getting accurate instructions past official notices. His courage does not make him comfortable with danger.

**Captain Halden**, Mara's former convoy commander, signed orders that sent people into the bell network. He knew prisoners were being transferred and ignored complaints about the receiving stations; he did not know the full feeding process. Learning it does not erase his responsibility. His slow break with Vale includes releasing a detained convoy, disobeying the gate closure, and eventually giving evidence under his own name.

**Sen, the Alder Fen ward coordinator**, already knows how to keep a small settlement safe. She refuses to let the party announce an evacuation until they can answer mundane questions: who takes the third watch, where children sleep, and how food arrives when a bridge floods. Her friendship with Soren grows through blunt disagreement and shared work.

**Orren** remains part of the story after his rescue. He has retained much of his practical craft knowledge even though parts of his personal history are gone. At a shelter workshop, he repairs lantern fittings and writes new instructions with pictures so an exhausted apprentice can follow them. He is free to stop working and sometimes does; surviving the bell has not made him responsible for saving everyone else.

**Neri** becomes an organizer in her own right. She decides where she lives and how she helps. Tavin's search brings the party to her, but her later choices are not a reward issued to him for finding her.

## The campaign route

The main route moves from Reedbank and Merewatch to the market town of Dunmere, the settlements of Alder Fen, and finally Veyr. Rest houses and supply stops change as this route opens. Travel still involves danger: a chain of warded shelters makes journeys manageable, not every stretch of road permanently safe.

The [world and bestiary](world-and-bestiary.md) divides that route into six exploration regions. Silt Crown guards the flooded granary, and the Gate-Censer guards a regional feeder approach. These encounters expand the existing main quests; optional rare hunts and equipment collections never become requirements for the evacuation.

## The four travelers

### Mara — the Warden

Mara once guarded Crown prisoner convoys. She was told the passengers would work off their sentences maintaining the bells. When a boy was added to her transport list without a charge, she refused the order and lost her commission.

She now takes escort work around Reedbank camp. Practical, watchful, and awkward around children, she repairs everyone's straps while claiming she only wants the rattling to stop.

At the bellworks, she recognizes the restraint fittings from her old convoys. Her arc is accepting responsibility without mistaking obedience for an excuse. By the end, she measures her oath by the people she protects, including people who cannot repay her and enemies who must still face judgment.

### Tavin — the Striker

Tavin is a courier who knows the abandoned roads better than the official maps. He claims he is looking for work. In truth, he has been following a transport route taken by his missing older sister, Neri.

He travels light, fights fast, and tells jokes precisely when everyone wishes he would be quiet. He keeps two spoons in his pack because Neri always forgot hers.

When he finds her, she is alive and building a new life, but she does not remember him. His story becomes more difficult than a rescue: learning to offer a relationship without demanding that she become the person he remembers.

### Iona — the Mender

Iona works at a roadside hospice where patients arrive without names. She gives each person a blank notebook and asks what they want written on its first page.

She joins the expedition to bring the missing workers back alive. She is warm, occasionally impatient, and fiercely protective of her cooking pot. Her humor is dry enough that Tavin often notices a joke ten minutes late.

Iona initially measures herself by what she can repair. She learns to accompany people through injuries she cannot reverse. Her hospice becomes the center of the network that shelters and trains evacuees.

### Soren — the Arcanist

Soren is a young bell engineer sent to inspect a fault in a mechanism he helped redesign. He believes the bells run on stored magical energy. His work made their consumption more efficient without showing him where the energy came from.

He can explain a pressure valve for twenty minutes and forget to mention that it is about to burst. He is also the first to stay up repairing a stranger's lantern.

Once he learns the truth, he wants to destroy every trace of his work. The others force him to confront a harder task: keeping people alive while helping replace the system he improved.

## The antagonist: First Warden Edran Vale

Vale commands the bell network from the capital, Veyr. Twenty years ago, he led an evacuation through the fog and brought back fewer than half the people who trusted him. Since then, he has made one promise: the capital's gates will never have to open at night.

He knows the bells consume prisoners' memories. He also knows their waste worsens the frontier fog. He calls it a postponement: one district suffers so a larger population survives another winter.

As the demand grows, petty offenders, debtors, and finally people with no recorded offense disappear into the system. Vale authorizes it. He is not merely being deceived by an advisor.

His strongest argument is practical. Small wards cannot shelter the capital's population, the roads are broken, and there is not enough food outside the walls. Initially, he is right about those obstacles. The party's campaign is the work of removing them.

When another option becomes possible, he still refuses. He cannot accept a plan that asks him to trust thousands of people instead of a mechanism he controls.

His defining exchange with Mara:

> **Vale:** “I know exactly how many people that bell keeps alive.”
>
> **Mara:** “Then learn the names of the people inside it.”

## The story

### Chapter One — A Bell Without a Voice

At Reedbank, the evening bell stops halfway through its warning. Its warning mechanism has failed, though the engine below is still feeding a weakening ward. The camp's keepers reinforce it with their small emergency ward, but cannot maintain that effort indefinitely. A maintenance crew has already gone missing beneath the nearby Merewatch tower.

Bessa assigns the travelers their work while arranging carts for a possible retreat. She gives Mara a list of the missing crew instead of a salvage inventory: “Bring these back before you bring back any tools.” A shared breakfast introduces the party through small frictions. Tavin pockets extra bread; Iona makes him wrap it properly; Soren forgets his own cup while explaining that the tower is perfectly safe to inspect.

Mara is hired to escort the repair team. Soren is the engineer assigned to the fault. Iona insists on coming when she sees that no medic has been included. Tavin offers a shortcut after recognizing the tower's seal on a scrap of his sister's transport papers.

In the bellworks, the party passes meal tins laid out for workers who never returned. A maintenance ledger lists people where Soren expects to find fuel deliveries. Most of the missing crew have already been transferred to the capital; one name, Orren, is marked as retained to keep the failing mechanism running.

One tin is painted with a crooked blue fish. Beside it lies a shift notebook in which someone has crossed out “reserve supply” and written “ask what this means.” These ordinary objects become the first evidence that the missing workers were investigating their own workplace.

At the bottom, a worker named Orren is trapped inside the binding frame of an animated brass bellkeeper. He is alive. Iona makes that clear before combat: they must break the outer mechanism, not attack the captive.

The party defeats the **Brass Bailiff**, a construct that announces a heavy strike by raising its bronze hammer and sounding a warning note. The damaged frame opens and Orren survives.

Disconnecting him stops Merewatch's remaining protection. Soren transfers the bell's finite stored energy into emergency lanterns, buying enough time to evacuate the exposed camp to the next warded shelter. This is a reserve, not an unlimited new power source.

The return is tense but brief. The party clears the road while Bessa gets the carts moving. Mara wants to leave the salvage behind; Soren argues for taking the diagrams because other towers may contain the same fittings. They agree to carry the records and emergency equipment, not the feeding apparatus. It is their first shared decision about what their journey is for.

Back at camp, Orren recognizes the tools in his hand but not the small painted bird attached to his coat. Iona writes down what he wants to remember from today.

Mara finds her former commander's seal on the binding order. Tavin finds Neri's name in the transfer ledger.

The travelers agree to follow the road together.

At the next shelter, the night watch changes while they eat. For the first time, Soren sees people maintain a small ward without any bell machinery. The scene does not solve the crisis; only a handful can rest beneath that roof. It gives him a question he will carry to Alder Fen.

### Chapter Two — The Names on the List

Following the transport route, the party reaches Dunmere, a prosperous market town whose streets are swept clean and whose hospice is overflowing. The bell rings on time. Nobody discusses the wagons arriving behind it.

At the gate, the party's papers name Orren as missing Crown property. The clerk Leth quietly changes the entry to “injured worker under care” and tells Iona which lodging house will admit him. Later, he asks to see the original ledger. He can trace its transfers, but warns that the first person accused of falsifying the accounts will be the junior clerk who copied them.

They help a clerk smuggle the original transfer records out of the counting house, then infiltrate an abandoned toll station being used as a holding site. Its guards include people who believe they are protecting prisoners from the fog; several help when confronted with evidence. Others choose their orders.

The records also contain Mara's signature on an old convoy receipt. She remembers the journey as a difficult assignment she completed without losing anyone on the road. Now she must confront what happened after delivery. She gives Leth permission to publish that page alongside the rest.

The station's impounding construct, the **Toll Collector**, treats every person without an approved departure token as cargo. Its rotating armor shutters force the party to choose between preparing protection and attacking an exposed mechanism. Disabling it frees the holding yard; no captive is inside its frame.

Here Tavin finds Neri. She has organized the other captives into a kitchen crew so everyone gets fed. She assumes he is another courier bringing flour.

He shows her the second spoon. She understands what it means to him, but it awakens no memory. She asks for time. He gives it to her.

That evening, Tavin prepares to follow Neri into the hospice. Iona asks whether she invited him. He stays at the door, then leaves a note saying where he can be found. The next morning Neri approaches him herself, asking for help moving flour sacks. They speak about the work in front of them before they attempt to speak about their childhood.

Soren discovers his own engineering marks on the station's feeders. The party escapes with the records and surviving captives, but publishing the evidence does not bring the Crown down. People are frightened. Many would rather believe the story is false than imagine a night without the bells.

Vale's administration controls gate permits, grain allotments, and the wardens who keep the roads open. Official notices call the captives volunteers and claim the rescued workers were poisoned by damaged equipment. Some townspeople believe the witnesses; others fear that admitting the truth will mean losing their homes. The disclosure creates allies and division rather than an instant uprising.

Leth keeps searching the original crew list. He traces the blue-fish tin's owner to a capital intake ward and records which other names have confirmed transfers. Their fate remains an explicit campaign objective.

Vale declares the party saboteurs. He privately offers Mara a pardon and safe quarters for the others if they surrender the records. She refuses.

His messenger carries a second envelope: a proposed increase in Dunmere's winter grain ration if the town's council repudiates the witnesses. The heroes leave understanding that proof alone cannot break his authority. They need somewhere people can actually go.

### Chapter Three — The Road Nobody Guards

The party seeks help from marsh settlements that survive beyond the great bells. Their small wards are kept alive by teams sharing short shifts. The settlements do not need outsiders to discover this practice; they need medicine, repaired crossings, and enough trained people to keep watch without starving their farms of workers.

Sen welcomes the refugees to Alder Fen, then refuses Soren's first proposal. His calculations assume that every trained caster is healthy, every shift changes on time, and every shelter is filled to its planned capacity. Sen asks him to show her the column for a feverish child whose parent cannot leave the bedside.

The party runs a supervised shelter trial with Dunmere volunteers. More people arrive than expected. The ward itself functions, but too few relief keepers are available, and crowding makes its boundary difficult to hold. The observers' emergency reserve and a controlled withdrawal prevent deaths. That reserve is spent; it cannot justify repeating the trial unchanged.

Nobody declares the whole idea impossible. Nobody calls the test a success. Soren stops trying to make one shelter serve as a small version of the capital. Sen proposes two refuges with staggered arrivals, duplicate crews, and a supply store at each.

The middle of the game turns the party's answer into something tangible. They reopen a flooded granary, clear poisoned creatures from an orchard, escort teachers between shelters, and repair an old chain of roadside rest houses. Each success creates a visible refuge and changes who can travel safely.

At the orchard, a Hush growth has formed the **Ashroot Hart**, an antlered shape that moves between contaminated trees. Its fight tests recovery from poison and restraint with spell charges after a longer expedition. Clearing the growth opens access to an existing clean storehouse and a route for supplies; it does not instantly heal the soil or make a harvest grow overnight.

Around the campfire afterward, Mara asks everyone to name one thing the revised plan assumes will go wrong. Tavin supplies six before she can stop him. Iona makes a list. The joke becomes a working habit: every shelter plan now includes a spare shift, a delayed cart, and someone too tired to do what was expected.

Soren compares bell waste collected along the roads with samples from the Hush. The connection becomes undeniable: the machines keep a town safe tonight while making the next town's tomorrow worse.

Mara's former soldiers arrive to arrest them. She shows their captain the records of people his unit transported. He does not immediately become their ally, but allows an evacuation to pass. Later, he returns with volunteers.

The captain is Halden. He asks Mara to keep his name out of the published orders in exchange for help. She refuses that condition while accepting the release of the detained travelers. When he returns, he brings his own signed statement as well as the volunteers. Trust begins with a specific action he cannot quietly undo.

By the chapter's end, the party has opened one continuous route to a region that can support more people. It is limited and hard won. It is enough to prove that another future is possible.

The revised shelter trial is the chapter's turning point. Crews change on schedule, an ill keeper is replaced, and the second refuge receives the overflow. Orren elects to attend, not as a power source but as a visitor. He sleeps through the changing watch. Iona leaves him asleep.

### Chapter Four — The City That Would Not Leave

Before entering Veyr, the party helps carry out staged shutdowns at the remaining regional towers. This is part of the main campaign. Each feeding chamber is retired only after its captives have been freed and its residents have replacement wards or a supported evacuation route. The work uses the shelter plan established in Alder Fen, with finite bell reserves buying time during each handover.

Dunmere provides the first major public test. Its council agrees to close the feeding chamber after Leth's witnesses testify and Sen's crews demonstrate the replacement arrangements. Bessa organizes receiving shelters; Halden's volunteers protect the departures. At the end of these coordinated handovers, reports confirm that Veyr is the last bell still feeding on living captives. The finale cannot reach this state through optional errands or a single remote switch.

The party then returns to Veyr with supplies, trained ward crews, witnesses, and a workable evacuation schedule. Iona's hospice and Leth have already built a quiet network inside the walls. Neri chooses to join it, organizing food distribution rather than returning to the front lines with Tavin.

Leth's work also leads them to the capital intake ward. They account for Merewatch's missing crew there. Several are alive and can leave with the first medical convoy; others died before the party arrived, and the records preserve their names. The worker who painted the blue fish has survived, but remembers nothing of painting it. They ask Orren to describe its terrible proportions and decide to keep the tin anyway. The rescue resolves the opening search without reversing its losses.

Vale finally meets the party face to face. He admits the truth. He offers to reduce the harvest, release some prisoners, and postpone the next expansion. He will not shut the central engine down.

The engine is close to requiring more captives than the frontier can provide. A newly signed order authorizes emptying the hospice wards. People who were supposedly being protected are becoming fuel.

Vale's bargain makes one last attempt to separate the party. He offers to clear Mara's service record, release Neri from all claims, fund Iona's hospice, and preserve Soren's career. Each refuses for a reason already established on the main route. Neri's freedom is not Vale's to bargain away, and one safe hospice would leave the other wards vulnerable.

When Vale closes the gates, the party's allies open the old service routes. The final chapter begins as an evacuation, not an attack on helpless city residents. Shops become supply points. Former enemies hold ward shifts beside refugees. The player sees people encountered earlier doing work the four heroes could never perform alone.

Halden openly disobeys the closure order. He does not command every soldier in the city: some lay down arms, some help civilians, and others defend the bell administration. The party's route avoids turning crowded streets into a battlefield. Their immediate opponents are the service tunnels' Hush and the engine's defensive constructs.

Bessa checks the last convoy lists against the shelter lists instead of merely counting wagons. Leth carries duplicate records outside the city before accompanying the final medical group. Sen assigns fresh ward crews to the receiving points. The evacuation's basic safety rests on these required main-story preparations. Side quests can change who else helps and what welcome awaits people, not whether the plan secretly works.

### Chapter Five — The Last Watch

The central engine must be shut down to end the feeding cycle, but simply breaking its core would collapse the capital's remaining protection. The party must reach its controls while the ward crews and rear guard move the last districts to safety.

In the final service passage, the **Night Choir**—three Hush gathered around a damaged resonance shaft—alternates attacks that silence casters with pressure on the whole party. The encounter gives the group's protective magic, recovery supplies, and physical attackers one last shared test. Winning it opens a stairwell that the rear guard can defend; it is not a separate magical cure for the fog.

At the stairwell, Neri tells Tavin she is leaving with the kitchen convoy. She has not come to discover that she was secretly brave in the past. She has chosen a job that frightened people need her to do now. He gives her the spare spoon. She asks him to bring back the other one.

Vale binds himself into the engine's command armor. He means to hold the system together by force, even if his own memories are the next to burn. This is desperation, not redemption; it does not cancel the people he sacrificed.

The final battle echoes the first. The party breaks the armor's external bindings and pulls a living person out of a machine. This time, that person is their enemy.

The automated engine continues fighting after its commander falls. Its final form is the **Heart Bell**, an immense suspended mechanism that turns the remaining stored memory residue into shields and weapons. The party disables its defenses while Soren diverts its last reserve into the evacuation wards.

Mara refuses to leave Vale inside. Iona stabilizes him. Tavin holds the stairwell while Soren completes the shutdown. The party escapes as the bell falls silent.

Outside the walls, the fog remains. The refugees' small wards hold. The next shift arrives before the first one collapses from exhaustion.

The world has not been cured in an instant. Its people have survived the first night without feeding another life into a bell.

At dawn, the party reaches the receiving shelter. Bessa is arguing about breakfast portions. Sen is asleep in a chair while a replacement makes her rounds. Someone has already started repairing the wheel on tomorrow's supply cart. The ordinary work continues before anyone finds time to call the travelers heroes.

## Ending

Months later, Veyr is smaller. Some districts remain abandoned. The roads between the settlements are guarded and repaired one stretch at a time. The Hush still inhabit the wilds, but no new bell waste is feeding them.

Vale survives to face a public hearing. The transfer records name both the missing and the officials responsible. Mara gives evidence about her own service instead of asking to be treated as an exception.

Soren teaches ward maintenance in a workshop with open doors. The plans are posted on the wall, where anyone can question them.

Iona's hospice keeps its notebooks. Beside each bed is space to write something that happened today.

Neri opens a roadside kitchen. Tavin brings flour, stays for supper, and learns which jokes she likes now. When they lay their two traveling spoons on the table, she puts a third beside them for Iona. Their relationship is new and real; her old memories have not returned.

Halden's statement is read at the same hearing as Mara's. Leth's records remain available to families, including records that make the new leaders uncomfortable. Bessa oversees the road shelters and still insists on counting people rather than carts. Sen returns to Alder Fen with a schedule that finally includes time off.

Optional quests add local scenes to this ending: a repaired ferry, a shared memorial, a new apprenticeship, a recipe passed on with permission. They do not restore burned memories, pardon Vale, or determine whether civilians deserved rescue. The central outcome is the same: the harvest has ended, responsibility is being examined, and rebuilding is shared work.

The last scene returns to the road outside Reedbank. A child hangs a little bronze bell beside a shelter lantern. It powers nothing. It is there so a traveler can call for help.

Under it is a painted sign:

> **Ring. Someone will come.**

## Opening dialogue for the first playable

At Reedbank's watchfire:

> **Camp keeper:** “Merewatch hasn't answered. If the reserve fails, we can't hold this many people here.”
>
> **Soren:** “A jammed regulator could explain it. The repair should be straightforward.”
>
> **Tavin:** “Excellent. I've always wanted to die somewhere straightforward.”
>
> **Iona:** “Bring a blanket, then. I refuse to carry you back cold.”
>
> **Mara:** “We find the crew first. Then we fix the bell.”

At the binding chamber:

> **Orren:** “Please. Don't let it ring again.”
>
> **Soren:** “There's someone inside the frame.”
>
> **Iona:** “He's breathing. Mara, the brass joints. Keep your blade away from the center.”
>
> **Mara:** “You heard her. We break the cage.”

After the rescue:

> **Orren:** “There was a name. I was keeping it safe.”
>
> **Iona:** “What would you like me to call you?”
>
> **Orren:** “Orren. I still have that.”
>
> **Iona:** “Then we'll start there.”

## First-playable boundary

The first 10–15-minute expedition covers Reedbank, the compact Merewatch bellworks, the Brass Bailiff, Orren's rescue, and the return to prepare the camp's evacuation. Its recovery point and supply vendor remain available after the boss, matching the combat brief's short expedition loop.

The camp evacuation can be a short closing scene rather than a new escort system. Later chapters are campaign story direction, not additional requirements for the first prototype.

Only the short camp task identified in the [side-quest collection](side-quests.md) is suitable as an optional addition to this first playable. The other quests belong to the expanded campaign. The opening rescue and evacuation are not delayed by a long excursion or a hidden real-time countdown.

Suggested opening quest: **The Silent Watch**. Objective sequence: speak to the keeper → enter the bellworks → find the missing crew → free Orren → return to Reedbank. The main campaign quest, **The Road Beyond the Bells**, begins when the party examines the transfer ledger.

## Optional stories and campaign pacing

Each companion has two optional episodes that develop a particular relationship or future role. Their essential decisions remain in the main chapters: Mara accepts responsibility, Tavin respects Neri's autonomy, Iona accepts that some losses are irreversible, and Soren helps replace the system he improved.

Regional quests create reasons to revisit places and recognize the people who live there. Dialogue and the epilogue acknowledge their outcomes. An unresolved optional quest leaves an ordinary local problem, not an unexplained failure of the mandatory evacuation.

The main route gives players quiet periods in supported shelters between urgent events. There is no implied hidden countdown while the party speaks to people. Before entering Veyr's final service passage, Bessa explicitly says that the last operation is beginning and offers a clear chance to finish unfinished business. Missable optional dialogue is never disguised as an essential ending requirement.

Useful recurring camp scenes include Tavin teaching Soren a traveling song with deliberately wrong directions, Iona finding Mara secretly patching everyone's coats, and Sen returning Soren's spotless staffing chart with soup spilled over the blank space where a dinner break should have been. Their friendship should be present in small scenes, not reserved for speeches before bosses.
