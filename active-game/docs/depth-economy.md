# Preparation and slower earnings

New ordinary enemies award 50% of their previous EXP contribution and 65% of their previous gold contribution, rounded down with a minimum contribution of one. Each fixed region keeps its own enemy strength, encounter roles, and stronger color variants. Enemy stats do not increase with the party's level.

Boss and rare encounter budgets retain 65% of their previous EXP and 75% of their previous gold. The complete encounter budget is reduced once, then allocated among its enemies. Multi-voice bosses therefore keep one encounter budget rather than multiplying it. A scripted encounter that previously awarded zero still awards zero.

Victory sums defeated contributions, divides EXP among living travelers with integer rounding, and pays gold once to the shared purse. Fallen travelers earn no EXP. Escape and defeat award neither EXP nor gold. Existing saves retain all previously earned EXP, gold, items, and learned spells. Equipment prices, spell prices, spell-tier unlocks, and level thresholds are unchanged.

## Measured opening preparation

Two deterministic combat/economy probes start fresh with ordinary starter resources, one with a Warden lead and one with an Arcanist lead. They clear the six authored opening encounters, rest at Reedbank's normal free shelter, and then earn the tested level-four preparation band through four additional encounters triggered by ordinary repeated walking. The six authored encounters alone now reach level three. This is a preparation option, not a required level gate.

Both parties buy four Potions for 120 gold, Spark for Soren and Shelter for Iona for 70 gold. The Arcanist also buys Frost for 35 gold. No EXP, money, gear, HP, or spell charges are injected. In the final assembled build, Brass Bailiff takes seven rounds for both parties. All four travelers survive. Their final funds are 463 and 444 gold respectively; this includes the reduced 150-gold boss reward. These funds leave room for further supplies, spell choices, and paid lodging.

Those focused probes use position fixtures to skip travel time between training and the shelter. They demonstrate earned-resource combat and purchasing feasibility, not average encounter frequency or a complete campaign playthrough.

## Actual three-floor expedition

A separate fresh Warden run uses 321 actual walking steps from character creation through the three Bellworks floors, then back to Reedbank and into the dungeon again through its opened service lift. It never changes player coordinates, EXP, gold, HP, inventory, or spell charges through a fixture. Walking encounters stay in the default classic mode. The route buys Spark, collects the marked once-only supply caches, reads the floor records, and latches the third-floor return lift. By then Soren has spent both starting spell charges, so the route retreats through the lift and the actual overworld entrances for normal camp recovery.

The party buys four Potions for 120 gold and Shelter for 35 gold with earned funds, returns through the unlocked lift, and defeats Bailiff in seven rounds. Eleven battles occur across the route, including the boss. All four survive at level four with 193 earned EXP each and 464 gold. The sample retains 13 Potions and five Antidotes; it did not need a curative on this seed. The lower first-dungeon pressure is deliberate introductory scope. This result demonstrates a working preparation, exploration, retreat, resupply, and return loop; it does not prove that grinding or medicine is mandatory for every strategy.

## Later combat bounds

The earned-resource second-region probe now uses repeatable walking encounters rather than repeatedly challenging a cleared authored patrol. Fourteen training fights reach level nine. The party pays for Dunmere lodging, buys its compatible regional weapons and body armor, and learns Storm Wave and Renew after they unlock. The Toll Collector takes nine rounds; all four travelers survive with 818 gold remaining. This probe uses position fixtures between services and fights, so it is combat/economy evidence rather than a full four-floor route.

Fifteen maximum-formation fixtures still test nine small enemies, four guards, and a mixed group in regions two through six, now at valid deep-floor locations. They use declared prepared levels, ordinary regional equipment, and unlocked learned spellbooks rather than claiming earned campaign progression. Later-floor attrition, low-level rare groups, and human resource choices still need broader playtesting. Rewards alone do not determine difficulty: formation roles, limited charges, curatives, optional caches, and shortcut discovery provide the expedition decisions.
